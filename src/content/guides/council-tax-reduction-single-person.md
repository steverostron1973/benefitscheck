Living alone often means two separate types of council tax support can apply — a discount simply for being a single occupant, and potentially Council Tax Reduction on top if your income is low. Many single people only claim one when they could be entitled to both. This guide explains the difference, what you could get, and how to make sure you're not missing out.

## The single person discount — not means-tested

If you're the only adult living in your home, you're entitled to a **25% discount** on your council tax bill. This is separate from Council Tax Reduction and isn't means-tested — it doesn't matter how much you earn or how much you have in savings. It's based purely on the number of adults resident in the property.

To qualify, you generally need to be the only person aged 18 or over living in the property as your main home. Certain people are "disregarded" for this purpose even if they do live with you — including full-time students, some carers, and people who are severely mentally impaired — which means you might still qualify for the single person discount even if you're not technically the only adult in the house.

**This discount is usually applied automatically** if the council has records showing only one adult at the address, but it's always worth checking your bill and contacting your council directly if you think you should be getting it but aren't.

## Council Tax Reduction — means-tested, on top of the discount

Council Tax Reduction (sometimes called Council Tax Support) is a separate, means-tested reduction based on your income, savings, and circumstances. As a single person, you can potentially receive this **in addition to** the 25% single person discount — the discount is applied first, and then Council Tax Reduction is calculated on the reduced bill.

Because Council Tax Reduction schemes are set locally by each council, the exact amount you could get, and the income thresholds involved, vary depending on where you live. Some councils' schemes can reduce a low-income single person's bill by a further significant amount on top of the 25% discount — in some cases down to little or nothing, particularly for pensioners or those on the lowest incomes.

## Who counts as "single" for these purposes

This is often more nuanced than people expect:

- If you live with a partner, you're not eligible for the 25% single person discount, even if only one of you works or has an income — you'd need to look at Council Tax Reduction as a couple instead
- If you live with adult children, friends, or lodgers, you may lose the single person discount unless those individuals fall into a "disregarded" category (such as being a full-time student)
- If you live alone but have a partner who doesn't live with you, you're still generally considered single for council tax purposes at your own address
- Recent bereavement, separation, or an adult child moving out can all be reasons your household composition — and therefore your entitlement — has changed, so it's worth reviewing your council tax situation after any of these events

## How to apply

- **Single person discount**: contact your council directly if it's not already showing on your bill — many councils have a simple online form for this
- **Council Tax Reduction**: apply separately through your local council's website; this isn't automatic just because you're claiming the single person discount or another benefit like Universal Credit

## Common mistakes

- **Only claiming the discount, not checking for Council Tax Reduction too.** Many single people on a low income are entitled to both but only claim the automatic discount.
- **Assuming a discount showing on your bill means you've claimed everything you're entitled to.** The 25% discount and Council Tax Reduction are assessed completely separately.
- **Not reviewing your entitlement after a change in circumstances.** Moving in with someone, having someone move out, retiring, or a change in income can all affect what you're entitled to — it's worth checking again rather than assuming your existing arrangement is still correct.
- **Not checking for disregarded persons.** If someone else lives with you but falls into a disregarded category (such as a full-time student), you may still be entitled to the single person discount even though technically more than one adult lives in the property.

## What to do if you disagree with a decision

If you believe your single person discount or Council Tax Reduction has been calculated incorrectly, first contact your council to ask them to review the decision. If you still disagree after their review, you can appeal to the Valuation Tribunal (Valuation Tribunal for England, or for Wales) within **two months** of the council's review decision. This is a different, independent process from your council itself, and separate from the Mandatory Reconsideration and tribunal system used for DWP benefits like PIP or Universal Credit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I get both the single person discount and Council Tax Reduction?**
Yes — they're separate and can both apply. The 25% discount is applied to your bill first, and Council Tax Reduction can then be assessed on top of that reduced amount if your income and circumstances qualify.

**Is the single person discount automatic?**
It's often applied automatically if your council's records show only one adult at the address, but it's always worth checking your bill and contacting your council if you think it's missing.

**What if someone moves in with me?**
You'll usually need to tell your council, since this could affect your single person discount and any Council Tax Reduction you receive. Some people who move in (such as full-time students) may not affect your discount if they're "disregarded" for council tax purposes.

**Does having a partner who doesn't live with me affect my single person status?**
Generally no — if your partner has their own separate address and doesn't live with you, you're usually still considered a single occupant at your own address for council tax purposes.

**I've just been widowed or separated — does this change my council tax?**
It could. If you're now the only adult living in the property, you may become entitled to the 25% single person discount for the first time, so it's worth reviewing your situation and contacting your council.

**Does my income affect the 25% single person discount?**
No — the discount is based purely on the number of adults in the property, not your income or savings. Council Tax Reduction is the separate, means-tested support that does depend on your income.

**What if my adult child still lives with me but is a full-time student?**
Full-time students are usually "disregarded" for council tax purposes, meaning you may still qualify for the single person discount even though another adult lives in the property.

**How do I appeal if I think a decision is wrong?**
Ask your council to review it first, then appeal to the Valuation Tribunal within two months of their review decision if you still disagree.
