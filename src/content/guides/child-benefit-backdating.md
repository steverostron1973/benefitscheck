If you've recently had a child, or only just found out you were eligible to claim, you might be wondering how far back your Child Benefit claim can go. This guide covers the standard backdating rules, plus a separate — and much longer-reaching — scheme for recovering lost National Insurance credits from years you didn't claim at all.

## Standard backdating: up to 3 months

For a normal new Child Benefit claim, HMRC can backdate your payments for **up to 3 months** from the date you submit your claim. You don't need to give a special reason for the delay — this backdating is automatic, as long as you were eligible for Child Benefit during that period.

For example, if your baby was born on 1 January and you claim on 1 March, your Child Benefit would be backdated to 1 January, since that's within the 3-month window.

If you delay claiming for longer than 3 months, you'll generally lose any payments beyond that window — there's no further backdating available for the payments themselves under the standard rules, which is why claiming promptly after your child is born or comes to live with you is worth doing even if you're busy with everything else that comes with a new baby.

## When can I make a claim?

You can claim Child Benefit as soon as **48 hours** after registering your child's birth, or as soon as a child comes to live with you in other circumstances (such as adoption or a child moving in with a relative). There's no need to wait until your child is a certain age.

## A separate issue: missed National Insurance credits from past years

Backdating payments is different from a separate, much bigger issue: if you **never claimed Child Benefit at all** in past years — perhaps because you assumed it wasn't worth it due to your income, or you simply didn't know you were eligible — you may have missed out on National Insurance credits for those years, which count towards your State Pension.

This is a serious gap for many parents, particularly those who stayed home to care for children without realising that not claiming Child Benefit (even the smallest amount, or opting out of payment) meant missing out on qualifying years towards their 35 years needed for a full State Pension.

## The retrospective National Insurance credit scheme

The government has been developing a specific route to let parents and carers apply retrospectively for the National Insurance credits they missed, covering years going back as far as **2013** (when the High Income Child Benefit Charge began, and many people mistakenly stopped claiming or never started).

**Important update on timing:** this scheme was originally announced to launch in **April 2026**, but has since been **delayed to April 2027**. If you're affected — particularly if you're close to claiming your State Pension and have gaps in your National Insurance record from years you didn't claim Child Benefit — it's worth being aware that the tool to apply retrospectively isn't yet available, and checking back closer to the revised date.

Once launched, the scheme is expected to allow retrospective claims back to 2013 as a one-off transitional arrangement, with an ongoing 6-year limit for retrospective claims after that.

## What to do in the meantime

- If you're currently eligible for Child Benefit and haven't claimed, claim now — the standard 3-month backdating rule and, more importantly, ongoing National Insurance credit protection, apply from the point you claim
- If you have gaps in your National Insurance record from years you didn't claim Child Benefit, keep an eye on GOV.UK and HMRC announcements for the retrospective credit scheme's revised launch
- If you're close to State Pension age and concerned about gaps, it may be worth checking your State Pension forecast and getting advice on whether paying voluntary National Insurance contributions makes sense in the meantime, rather than waiting for the retrospective scheme

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing the two different backdating issues.** Backdating your actual payments is capped at 3 months; recovering lost National Insurance credits from years you didn't claim at all is a separate, longer-reaching issue with its own (delayed) process.
- **Assuming the retrospective NI credit scheme is already available.** As of the most recent update, it's delayed to April 2027 — don't assume you can apply for historical credits right now.
- **Delaying a current claim while waiting for backdating rules to somehow extend further.** The 3-month rule for payments is fixed — delaying a new claim only loses you money, since there's no benefit to waiting.
- **Not checking your State Pension forecast if you have gaps.** Even before the retrospective scheme launches, it's worth understanding your current position so you can make informed decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

**How far back can I backdate a new Child Benefit claim?**
Up to 3 months from the date you submit your claim, without needing a special reason.

**Can I get National Insurance credits backdated further than 3 months?**
Not through the standard claim process — but a separate scheme is being introduced to let you apply retrospectively for NI credits from years you didn't claim, covering years back to 2013. This has been delayed from its original April 2026 launch to April 2027.

**When exactly does the retrospective NI credit scheme launch?**
As of the most recent confirmed update, it's expected in April 2027, having been delayed from its original April 2026 target.

**Will I need to apply for the retrospective credits, or will they be added automatically?**
Based on current plans, you'll need to actively apply once the scheme launches — it won't be automatic.

**What if I'm about to claim my State Pension and have gaps from not claiming Child Benefit?**
It's worth checking your State Pension forecast now, and considering advice on voluntary National Insurance contributions, since the retrospective scheme isn't available yet.

**Does the 48-hour rule mean I have to wait 48 hours to claim?**
No — it means you can claim as soon as 48 hours after registering the birth; it's not a required waiting period, just the earliest point HMRC's systems allow a claim.

**If I claim now, will my ongoing National Insurance credits be protected going forward?**
Yes — claiming now protects your ongoing National Insurance credits from this point forward; it's specifically the *historical* gap from previous unclaimed years that requires the separate retrospective scheme.

**Where can I check for updates on the retrospective credit scheme?**
Keep an eye on GOV.UK and HMRC announcements, since the launch date has already shifted once and further updates may follow.
