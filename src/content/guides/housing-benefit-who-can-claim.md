Housing Benefit used to be the main way people on a low income got help with rent — but it's been closed to most new working-age claimants for years, replaced by the housing element of Universal Credit. This leaves a lot of confusion about who can actually still make a new claim. This guide explains exactly who qualifies, and what to do if you don't.

## The short answer

For most working-age people, **Housing Benefit is no longer available for new claims**. If you need help with rent and you're working age, you'll almost always need to claim the housing element of Universal Credit instead.

You can still make a **new** claim for Housing Benefit if you fall into one of these groups:

- You've reached **State Pension age**, and (if you have a partner) both of you have reached State Pension age
- You live in **supported, sheltered, or temporary accommodation** — this applies regardless of your age
- You're part of a small number of **protected "mixed-age" couples**, where the older partner reached State Pension age and was already receiving Housing Benefit or Pension Credit before 15 May 2019

## Pension-age claimants

If you're single and have reached State Pension age, you can make a new claim for Housing Benefit in the normal way — it isn't affected by the Universal Credit rollout in the same way working-age claims are.

If you're part of a couple and both of you have reached State Pension age, the same applies. However, if only one of you has reached State Pension age (a "mixed-age couple"), the rules are more restrictive — in most cases, you'll need to claim Universal Credit as a couple instead, unless you fall under the protected group described below.

## Mixed-age couples

Since 15 May 2019, mixed-age couples (where one partner is State Pension age and the other isn't) have generally needed to claim Universal Credit rather than Housing Benefit and Pension Credit, even though one partner has reached pension age.

There's an exception: if you were already receiving Housing Benefit or Pension Credit as a couple before 15 May 2019, and have continued to do so since, you may be protected and able to remain on these benefits rather than being moved onto Universal Credit. If you're in this situation and unsure of your status, it's worth checking with a welfare rights adviser before making any change that could affect your protection.

## Supported, sheltered, and temporary accommodation

Regardless of your age, you can make a new claim for Housing Benefit if you live in:

- **Specified or supported accommodation** — housing provided by a charity, housing association, or similar body that also provides care, support, or supervision
- **Sheltered housing**
- **Temporary accommodation** — short-term accommodation provided by a council, such as a hostel or bed and breakfast, often while homeless or waiting for a settled housing solution

This is because Universal Credit's housing element doesn't cover these accommodation types in the same way, so Housing Benefit remains the correct route for help with rent in these circumstances, whatever your age.

## If you're already on Housing Benefit as a working-age claimant

If you're working age and already receiving Housing Benefit, you haven't necessarily lost it — but the "managed migration" process to Universal Credit has been rolling out across the country, and most remaining working-age Housing Benefit claimants are expected to have moved onto Universal Credit by around mid-2026. If you receive a migration notice letter from the DWP, you'll need to claim Universal Credit by the deadline given, or your existing benefits (including Housing Benefit) will stop.

## Savings and capital limits

To qualify for Housing Benefit, your savings and capital generally need to be under **£16,000**, unless you receive the Guarantee Credit element of Pension Credit, in which case there's no upper capital limit for Housing Benefit purposes.

Between certain thresholds, a "tariff income" is assumed from your savings, which can reduce your award even if you're still under the capital limit — the exact thresholds differ slightly depending on whether you're working age or pension age.

## What to do if you're not eligible for Housing Benefit

If you're working age and don't fall into one of the exceptions above, you'll need to claim the housing element of Universal Credit instead. See our guide on [Housing Benefit and Universal Credit](/guides/housing-benefit-universal-credit/) for how the two systems relate and what changes when you move between them.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming Housing Benefit is completely gone.** It still exists for pension-age claimants and those in supported/temporary accommodation — it's only closed to most new working-age claims.
- **Not checking mixed-age couple protection.** If you were already receiving Housing Benefit or Pension Credit as a couple before May 2019, you may still be protected — don't assume you automatically need to move to Universal Credit.
- **Ignoring a migration notice letter.** If you receive one, you must act by the deadline given, or your existing benefits (including Housing Benefit) will stop, potentially leaving a gap before your Universal Credit payments begin.
- **Assuming savings over the limit rule out help entirely.** If you receive Guarantee Pension Credit, there's no upper savings limit for Housing Benefit — it's worth checking this specific interaction rather than assuming you don't qualify.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I make a brand new claim for Housing Benefit if I'm working age?**
Generally no, unless you live in supported, sheltered, or temporary accommodation. Otherwise, you'll need to claim the housing element of Universal Credit instead.

**I'm about to reach State Pension age — can I claim Housing Benefit then?**
Yes, if you're single (or in a couple where both of you have reached State Pension age), you can make a new claim for Housing Benefit once you reach pension age.

**What happens if I get a migration notice letter?**
You need to claim Universal Credit by the deadline stated in the letter, or your current benefits — including Housing Benefit — will stop. Act on it promptly rather than leaving it.

**Does living in supported accommodation mean I can claim Housing Benefit at any age?**
Yes — supported, sheltered, and temporary accommodation are exceptions to the working-age restriction, regardless of your age.

**What if my partner is State Pension age but I'm not?**
In most cases, you'll need to claim Universal Credit as a couple rather than Housing Benefit, unless you're part of a protected group that was already receiving these benefits before 15 May 2019.

**Do savings affect my Housing Benefit eligibility?**
Yes — generally you need savings under £16,000, though this doesn't apply if you receive the Guarantee Credit element of Pension Credit.

**If my Housing Benefit stops when I move to Universal Credit, can I go back?**
Generally no — once you've moved to Universal Credit, you won't usually be able to reclaim Housing Benefit unless your circumstances change significantly (such as moving into supported or temporary accommodation).

**Where do I apply for Housing Benefit if I qualify?**
Housing Benefit is administered by your local council, not centrally through GOV.UK or the DWP — contact your council directly to apply.
